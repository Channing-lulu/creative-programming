## How to use ai
### What I use
*chatgpt* *deepseek*
maybe *claude* *gpt-6* *banana* for future assignments

### What is made by AI in readme
I wrote the outline and core arguments of the essay myself — this can be verified in the commit history of the README. I then expanded my ideas into a rough draft in Chinese, using my own words to articulate each point. After that, I used an AI language model to polish the language and improve clarity, while strictly following the structure and arguments I had already defined. I reviewed every section the AI generated to ensure it accurately reflected my original intent. Finally, I asked the AI to translate the essay into English and to help me check for any logical gaps or inconsistencies.

### Things I kept
I kept a significant portion of the AI's phrasing and sentence-level refinements, as it helped make my arguments more articulate and readable. I also trusted the AI's translation capability — but only on the condition that it did not alter the substance of my content. I carefully compared the translated version against my original Chinese draft to confirm that my arguments remained intact.
### Things I rejected
Whenever I found that the AI had modified my core arguments, inserted claims I did not agree with, or added content that did not represent my position, I rejected those changes entirely. I deleted those sections and restored my original wording. I also rejected any overly generic or clichéd phrasing that sounded polished but lacked substance. The final essay reflects my own thinking — the AI was used for language refinement and translation, not for generating ideas.
